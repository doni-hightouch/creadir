# Education center — research corpus index

Research gathered for the Creadir education center (planned: book icon, top
right, opens an interactive learning center for Hightouch Ad Studio). **Nothing
built yet — this is the knowledge base only.**

## The five source files

1. **[ad-studio-manual.md](ad-studio-manual.md)** — Ad Studio's own account of
   its workflow, stage by stage (setup → brief → prompting → generation →
   revision → styles → finishing → boundaries). Self-reported, so it's the
   *how it works* layer with rules of thumb and good/bad input pairs.
2. **[customer-reality.md](customer-reality.md)** — the honest internal
   diagnosis of where users fail, with named accounts and verbatim quotes, plus
   the five behaviors designers *should* adopt and the live-workshop playbook.
   **The most important file** — it defines what the curriculum must fix.
3. **[teaching-demo-arc.md](teaching-demo-arc.md)** — how Ad Studio is explained
   to a cold audience: the market thesis, positioning sentences, the 80/20
   pitch, guardrails-as-a-dial, the designer-replacement reframe, and the real
   one-sentence demo prompts.
4. **[teaching-onboarding-calls.md](teaching-onboarding-calls.md)** — the
   hands-on onboarding flow: the junior-designer model, the layers of
   guardrails, the prompting curriculum in taught order, and the decomposing
   confusion that reads as a bug.
5. **[teaching-enablement-sessions.md](teaching-enablement-sessions.md)** —
   enablement with real customers: the three-layer governance model
   (brand context → styles → no-style), references as the highest-leverage
   input, the "riff" lesson, the full prompt toolkit, and the five predictable
   customer gaps.
6. **[teaching-coaching-curriculum.md](teaching-coaching-curriculum.md)** —
   what gets taught when real work goes wrong: encode corrections rather than
   re-prompting, "read this when X" descriptions, strictness calibration,
   bad-first-batch expectation, chats-don't-share-memory, and the nine failure
   modes to design around.

## The through-line across all sources

The problem is **not** that users can't write prompts. It's that they lack a
mental model of (a) what the system already knows and (b) what their own job is.
Every documented failure is one of those two halves missing.

Three ideas recur in every single source and should anchor the curriculum:

1. **Art-direct, don't specify.** Treat it as a junior designer you assign work
   to — give direction plus deliberate ambiguity. This is the documented moment
   of unlock even for an AI-fluent power user.
2. **References beat descriptions** — "the easiest, heaviest hitting, best way"
   — and you must say *what* you liked about the reference.
3. **Ask for volume.** Ten at a time. More shots on goal. Non-obvious to
   everyone, taught by everyone.

Plus the meta-lesson that makes the portal necessary at all: knowledge trapped
in one person's chat doesn't transfer. Consistency between an expert operator
and a novice is *the* named unsolved problem.
