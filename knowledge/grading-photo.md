# How Photo Competition Judges Actually Score

## 1. How the pros score

**PPA (IPC / merit competitions) — 100-point scale, panel of 5 jurors, scores averaged.**

| Band | Label | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 95–100 | Exceptional | Near-flawless; strong in all 12 elements; award contender |
| 90–94 | Superior | Distinguished; excels in most elements |
| 85–89 | Excellent | Clearly above merit; excels in several elements |
| 80–84 | Deserving of a Merit | The bar. Competent in ALL 12 elements, strong in some |
| 75–79 | Above Average | Good professional work with one or more visible weaknesses |
| 70–74 | Average | Ordinary professional work; nothing elevates it |
| 65–69 | Below Exhibition Standards | Fails basic craft or presentation |

- **Merit threshold = 80.** Below 80, no merit, no credit toward degrees.
- Key insight: 75–79 is *good professional work*. Merit means better than everyday professional standard.
- Jurors score silently by keypad. Any juror can **challenge** a score; a spread of 10+ points from the average forces an automatic challenge. The challenger states the reason aloud, panel discusses, image is rescored.
- An 81 vs. an 83 is noise. Band changes (79 → 80) are what matter.

**Camera clubs — 3 judges, small scales, summed or averaged.**
- Common: each judge scores 3–9 (or 5–9), total out of 27. 27 = best in show territory; 21–23 = strong; 18–20 = typical middle; below 18 = clear flaws.
- Many clubs (e.g., FCCC) split the judge's score into three explicit buckets: **impact, composition, technique** — weak in one bucket caps the total.
- Guidance to judges: an image satisfying only a few criteria scores low; satisfying some scores mid; standing out in *all three* buckets scores high.

**FIAP international salons — 3 judges, 1–5 each, max 15.**
- Scoring is pass/fail in effect: the panel sets a cut score so **acceptances ≤ ~30% of entries** (FIAP rule). "Acceptance" is the unit of credit toward AFIAP/EFIAP distinctions.

## 2. The 12 elements, distilled (PPA)

1. **Impact** — the image provokes an immediate emotional reaction.
2. **Technical excellence** — exposure, sharpness, color, retouching, output quality are clean.
3. **Creativity** — a fresh, original way of seeing, not a copied recipe.
4. **Style** — a recognizable, consistent artistic voice, appropriate to the subject.
5. **Composition** — every visual element is placed to serve the intent and guide the eye.
6. **Presentation** — mat, border, and display enhance the image, never fight it.
7. **Color balance / harmony** — tones work together to support the mood.
8. **Center of interest** — the eye goes where the maker intended, and stays.
9. **Lighting** — light is *controlled*: it sculpts shape, dimension, and mood.
10. **Subject matter** — the subject fits and carries the story.
11. **Technique** — the chosen methods (posing, capture, processing) are executed well.
12. **Storytelling** — the image invites the viewer's imagination; it says something.

Rule of thumb from judge training: a merit image is **competent in all 12 and strong in several**. One hard failure in any element keeps the image under 80 no matter how strong the rest is.

## 3. What earns points, what loses points

**Earns points**
- Light with direction and purpose: shadow that models form, clean highlight-to-shadow transitions.
- A single unambiguous center of interest; the eye enters, travels, returns.
- Original concept or viewpoint on a familiar subject.
- Emotional pull within the first two seconds of viewing.
- Color palette that is deliberate and unified.
- Flawless finish: clean retouching, correct skin tones, sharp where it must be sharp.
- Presentation (border/mat/keyline) that quietly amplifies the image.

**Loses points (the usual verbalized deductions)**
- Blown highlights or blocked shadows with no detail.
- Mergers: background lines intersecting heads, limbs cut at joints, subject touching frame edge.
- Distractions: bright spots at edges, clutter, stray objects pulling the eye off the subject.
- Flat or unmotivated light; hot spots on skin.
- Heavy-handed retouching: plastic skin, halos, over-sharpening, visible composite edges.
- Soft focus where it matters (eyes).
- Cliché subject handled ordinarily — impact and creativity scores collapse.
- Tilted horizon, careless crop, weak subject placement.
- Presentation errors: distracting mat/border, poor print quality, color cast from bad calibration.

**How a judge verbalizes 82 → 76:** they name the failing element, concretely. "The highlight on the cheek is gone." "The branch merges with her head." "The crop amputates the hand." "Beautiful light, but I've seen this image a thousand times." Each named flaw drops the image one band; the score follows the worst credible criticism the panel accepts.

## 4. Anchors

- **95 image:** stops the room; original concept, sculpted light, flawless craft, perfect presentation — nothing a juror can name to take away.
- **80 image:** solid story, controlled light, clean technique, no notable flaw — but nothing extraordinary either; it earns the merit on competence.
- **70 image:** competent snapshot-plus: correct exposure, ordinary light, ordinary idea, at least one visible flaw (distraction, soft focus, weak crop).

## 5. Distribution reality

- **FIAP salons:** capped at ~30% acceptance by rule; most entries are rejected.
- **PPA IPC:** no quota — merit is threshold-based, but in practice most first-time entrants merit few or none of their four entries; seasoned competitors aim for "going 4-for-4" as a notable achievement. Scores of 90+ are rare; 95+ is exceptional by definition.
- **Camera clubs (27-point):** scores cluster in the 18–23 band; perfect 27s are reserved for one or two images a season.
- Practical takeaway: the median serious entry lands in "above average" (75–79) — good work that fails on exactly one nameable element.
