# How Advertising Is Formally Scored: Mechanics and Distributions

## 1. The real distributions

**System1 Test Your Ad** (1.0–5.9 star scale, emotion-based, 150,000+ ad database)
- Only **1%** of ads score 5 stars.
- Only **~17%** of ads reach 3 stars or above. So **~83%** sit below "good."
- The majority of all ads land in the **1–2 star range** (low/modest growth potential).
- The average US TV ad sits in the 2-star range.
- The average ad gets a **neutral (no emotion) response from ~47–52%** of viewers. Dullness is the default state.
- Ads with the fewest right-brain features: **60%+ score just 1 star** (Orlando Wood, *Lemon*, IPA).

**Cannes Lions** (26,900 entries in 2025)
- Roughly **10%** of entries make the shortlist.
- Roughly **3–4%** win any Lion. Verified per-category: 469 entries → 16 Lions (3.4%); 735 entries → 22 Lions (3.0%).
- Grand Prix: usually **1 per category**, ~0.1–0.2% of entries.

**D&AD** (11,689 entries in 2025)
- **668 Pencils awarded = ~5.7%** of entries win anything.
- Breakdown: Wood 434 (~3.7%), Graphite 176 (~1.5%), Yellow 48 (**~0.4%**), White 3, Black 3 (**~0.03%**).
- No quotas. If nothing deserves a Pencil, none is given. Some years have zero Black Pencils.

**Kantar Link** (260,000+ ad database)
- Scores are percentile ranks against norms, so the distribution is defined: top 30% / middle 40% / bottom 30%.
- A "high" ad is literally top-30-percentile. Most ads are, by construction, unremarkable.

Common shape across all systems: **great is 1–6%, genuinely good is 10–20%, the fat middle-to-bottom is 80%+.**

## 2. What the scoring systems measure

**System1**
- **Star Rating** (1.0–5.9): long-term brand growth potential. Driven by intensity of positive emotion (happiness, surprise) vs neutrality, captured via FaceTrace emotional response.
- **Spike Rating**: short-term sales effect (8–10 weeks). Strength of branding × emotional intensity. Also the tiebreaker among 5.9-star ads.
- **Fluency Rating** (0–100): speed and accuracy of brand recognition.

**Kantar Link / LINK+**
- Four core components: **Engagement, Brand Associations, Short-Term Sales Impact, Long-Term Brand Equity Contribution.**
- Diagnostics: **branding, enjoyment, involvement, persuasion** (persuasion = lift in short-term purchase intent), plus executional response.
- Validated finding: **8 of 10 ads in the top 30% for branding, enjoyment, or persuasion** are also predicted high performers overall.
- Top STSL ads generate **~33% more sales** than average (index 133).

**Ipsos Creative|Spark**
- **Branded Attention**: capturing and holding attention in a distracted environment, with the brand attached.
- **Creative Effect Index**: sales-validated short-term lift.
- **MISFITS framework**: three experiences — Creative Experiences, Empathy & Fitting In, Creative Ideas. All three together = **+20% effectiveness lift**.

**DAIVID**
- Four pillars: **Attention → Emotion → Memory → Intention.**
- Measures: % feeling intense emotion (39 distinct emotions, second-by-second), attention in first/last seconds, correct brand recall %, purchase intent.
- One composite effectiveness score out of 10.

**Cannes / D&AD juries**
- Cannes: jurors score 1–9 on published category criteria (typical split: idea, strategy, execution, results — weights vary by Lion, e.g. Creative Effectiveness weights results heavily). Averaged scores cut the shortlist. In-room debate plus **two-thirds majority vote** for each trophy.
- D&AD: multi-round jury deliberation over three days. Question is "is this good enough for a Pencil," not "what's the best here."

## 3. What separates a top ad from an average one

- **Emotion vs neutrality.** Average ads leave ~half of viewers feeling nothing. Top ads make most viewers feel something strong and positive.
- **Right-brain features.** Characters, dialogue, humor, story, music, a sense of place, "betweenness" of people. Their absence almost guarantees a 1-star score.
- **Branding fused with the enjoyment.** Top ads are recognized as the brand quickly; the entertaining part is inseparable from the brand (fluency devices, distinctive assets).
- **Early and late attention.** Top ads hook in the first seconds and land the brand at the end.
- **A single clear creative idea.** Juries reward one sharp idea executed with craft, not many competent elements.
- **Both effects at once.** The best ads score on short-term persuasion AND long-term emotion. Average ads pick one or neither.

## 4. Implications for building an honest grading rubric

- **Put the true center low.** The median real-world ad is a 2 out of 5, not a 3.5. A rubric where "average" means "pretty good" is lying.
- **Make the top grade rare by design.** Calibrate: top grade ≈ top 1–5% (5-star / Pencil territory), not top quartile.
- **Score against a norm, not against the batch.** Kantar-style percentiles beat "best of what's in front of you." No quotas — some batches earn zero top grades (D&AD rule).
- **Gate the top grades on emotion.** If the ad evokes nothing, cap it at 2/5 regardless of craft. Dullness is disqualifying, and it is the most common failure.
- **Gate on branding.** Strong emotion with weak brand attribution is a failed ad. Both must clear a bar before high scores unlock.
- **Score few dimensions, weighted.** Every validated system converges on ~4: attention/engagement, emotion/enjoyment, branding/memory, persuasion/intent. Idea and craft are jury add-ons.
- **Separate short-term and long-term.** One score for immediate persuasion, one for brand-building emotion. Averaging them hides which job the ad does.
- **Require supermajority conviction for the top grade.** Cannes needs two-thirds of a jury. If you hesitate, it's not a top ad.
