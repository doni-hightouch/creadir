# How Practitioners Tear Down and Improve Social Ads

A field guide for beginners. Distilled from Harry Dry (Marketing Examples), performance-creative strategists, and large-scale DTC static ad studies.

---

## 1. The core ideas (learn these first)

**Angle > hook > format.** The *angle* is the reason someone should care (the problem, fear, or desire you target). The *hook* is the first line or image that stops the scroll. The *format* is the container (testimonial, meme, comparison table). Angle changes move performance most. Test angles first, hooks second, formats third.

**Diversity beats volume.** Ten lazy variations of one ad teach you nothing. Ten genuinely different angles teach you everything. Top DTC teams ship 12–15 *new* statics a week across 6–8 formats at once.

**Ugly often wins.** Polished design screams "ad," and people skip ads. Ads that look like a screenshot, a text message, or a friend's post slip past the ad filter. Rule of thumb: keep ~80% on-brand, spend ~20% testing ugly.

**Specificity beats cleverness.** "Loved by thousands" is noise. "4,812 five-star reviews" is proof. If a claim can't be checked, readers discount it.

**When something wins, iterate in a tree.** Keep the winning angle. Branch it: new hook, new format, new persona, new visual style. Never just recolor the winner.

## 2. Harry Dry's three copy tests

Run every headline through these. If it fails one, rewrite.

1. **Can I visualize it?** If the reader can't picture it, they won't remember it.
2. **Can I falsify it?** Vague claims ("best quality") bounce off. Checkable claims ("ships in 24 hours") stick.
3. **Can anyone else say it?** If a competitor could paste in their logo, the line says nothing.

Bonus Dry rules: cut adjectives; write like a text to a friend; let customers write your copy (steal their review language verbatim); make one bold claim, then bury the reader in proof.

## 3. Copy formulas that survive on social

- **PAS (Problem–Agitate–Solve).** Name the pain. Twist the knife. Present the fix. Works because people act to end pain, not to gain features.
- **BAB (Before–After–Bridge).** Show life with the problem. Show life without it. The product is the bridge.
- **Show the receipt.** Don't say it works — show the screenshot, the review, the lab result, the number. Proof outperforms promises.
- **Claim + reason why.** Every claim earns more belief with a "because."
- **Customer-voice headline.** A real quote, typos and all, beats a written slogan.

## 4. The teardown method

The 6 steps practitioners use to critique any social ad:

1. **The 2-second test.** Cover everything but the image and first line. Would a stranger stop scrolling? If no, nothing else matters.
2. **Name the angle.** What problem or desire is this ad aimed at? If you can't say it in one sentence, the ad is unfocused.
3. **Check clarity.** Can a tired person on a phone tell what the product is and who it's for in 3 seconds? Squint test: does the key message survive as a thumbnail?
4. **Check the claims.** Visualizable? Falsifiable? Unique? Kill adjectives and jargon. Replace vague words with numbers.
5. **Count the proof.** Reviews, stats, before/afters, screenshots, endorsements. Zero proof elements = weak ad. Bold claim + heavy proof = strong ad.
6. **Check the ask.** Is there one clear, low-friction next step? "Learn more" is not a reason to click. Then prescribe one change at a time — new hook, new proof, or new angle — so you learn what moved the number.

## 5. 12 proven static ad formats

Each: **Name** — why it converts — *prompt-ready phrase for image generation.*

1. **Us-vs-them table** — anchors you against something buyers already dislike. — *"Comparison table, our product's green checkmarks versus competitor's red X's"*
2. **Testimonial static** — a stranger's words are trusted more than yours. — *"Customer review screenshot, five stars, product photo beside it"*
3. **Founder note** — a human explaining why they built it builds trust fast. — *"Typed founder letter on plain background, signature, small product photo"*
4. **Feature callout diagram** — annotations feel informational, not promotional. — *"Product photo with arrows and labels pointing to key benefits"*
5. **Before/after split** — makes the transformation visible and instant. — *"Split image, messy before on left, clean after on right"*
6. **Text-message screenshot** — looks like a friend's recommendation, not an ad. — *"iPhone text conversation screenshot recommending the product, casual tone"*
7. **Listicle ("5 reasons")** — lists are scannable and promise complete information. — *"Bold numbered list of five benefits next to product image"*
8. **Stat hero** — one specific number is more believable than any adjective. — *"Single giant statistic in bold type above small product shot"*
9. **Ugly / lo-fi note** — breaks the polished-ad pattern, so it gets read. — *"Handwritten sticky note about the product, photographed on a desk"*
10. **Meme format** — processed as entertainment first, so it earns free reach. — *"Popular two-panel meme layout, relatable product joke, casual captions"*
11. **Offer/promo card** — urgency plus a clear deal converts warm buyers. — *"Bold discount announcement, large percentage, deadline, single call-to-action button"*
12. **Grid collage** — multiple angles trigger completeness and longer dwell time. — *"Three-by-three collage of product, lifestyle shots, and review snippets"*

Match format to funnel stage: memes, ugly notes, screenshots up top (cold); comparisons, before/afters, listicles in the middle; offers, clean product shots, testimonials at the bottom (warm).

---

## 6. Quick rules to remember

- Hook first: the headline/image does 80% of the work.
- One ad, one angle, one message, one ask.
- Write the copy before you design the image.
- Steal words from real customer reviews.
- Proof over promises. Numbers over adjectives.
- If it looks like an ad, it gets skipped.
- Winners get branched, not retired: same angle, new hook, new format.
